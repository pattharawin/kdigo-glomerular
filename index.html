Supportive treatment review

Consider these elements alongside disease-specific therapy.

  • Monitor BP, eGFR, potassium, albumin and quantitative proteinuria; establish a follow-up interval and remission/relapse criteria.
  • Dietary sodium restriction and edema management as appropriate. Do not prescribe blanket high-protein supplementation for nephrotic protein losses; individualize protein intake to CKD stage and nutritional state.
  • ACE inhibitor OR ARB for hypertension/proteinuric disease when suitable; avoid combined ACEi+ARB, assess pregnancy and monitor creatinine/potassium after initiation or titration.
  • Review cardiovascular risk, lipids, smoking cessation and vaccination status; use statins where indicated.
  • Immunosuppression safety: TB/HBV/HCV screening where appropriate; CBC, liver tests, glucose, bone protection, infection and Pneumocystis jirovecii prophylaxis according to regimen and local protocol.
  • Lupus nephritis: consider hydroxychloroquine if not contraindicated, retinal monitoring, and measures to prevent flares and complications.
  • Pneumocystis jirovecii pneumonia (PJP) prophylaxis: consider TMP-SMX for high-dose prednisone and/or rituximab/cyclophosphamide per KDIGO 2021 Practice Point 1.8.4; check allergy, CBC, eGFR, potassium, interactions and local dose schedule. Alternative prophylaxis if sulfa-allergic.
  • SLE antimalarial: consider hydroxychloroquine (HCQ; preferred over chloroquine/CQ unless a specific reason dictates otherwise) if not contraindicated. Weight-based long-term target approximately ≤5 mg/kg/day actual weight, usual maximum 400 mg/day: for 65 kg, ceiling ≈325 mg/day before formulation and renal-risk review. This is not an automatic prescription.
  • HCQ/CQ retinal safety: refer for baseline ophthalmology exam (fundus plus OCT and FAF). Follow current eye-screening guidance; annual testing can be deferred for up to 5 years only when no significant risk factors, while CKD, high dose, older age, tamoxifen or CQ use warrant closer review.
